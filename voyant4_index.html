Nine Days in Suspended Limbo: The Material Reality of Detention

Before defining any technical abstraction, we anchor our inquiry in historical reality. In May 2019, inside the Guarded Centre for Foreigners (SOdC) in Poland, nine official Border Guard memorandums recorded a young woman's acute physical and psychic crisis[cite: 10, 15].

DAY 1 · MAY 17, 10:22 AM Sitting motionless in the hallway before the Provost's window; spitting toothpaste to scrawl "Iraq" onto the bare concrete floor; dismantling mattress covers and concealing a metal spoon[cite: 10, 15].
DAY 2 · MAY 17/18 (NIGHT) Tearing directional signage from the washroom door; responding to official lectures on institutional discipline with detached laughter[cite: 10, 15].
DAY 3 & 4 · MAY 18–19 Scavenging discarded food scraps; carrying an iron pan into the ward; kicking the sluice security gate and knocking over laundry machines; writing "Glass hate" in toothpaste across the window[cite: 10, 15].
DAY 5 & 7 · MAY 20–21 Holding knives at breakfast; leaving water taps gushing indefinitely; throwing boiled eggs and tomatoes at the guarded entry following a psychiatric session; writing "Death to everyone always" on room 041's window[cite: 10, 15].
DAY 8 & 9 · MAY 22–23 Gathering wild mushrooms and cigarette butts; smearing jam and cheese spread across walls; subdued by coercive force and transferred permanently to an isolated solitary cell under 15-minute surveillance[cite: 10, 15].

Theoretical Foundation

What is Voyant Tools? Agile Hermeneutics & Enhanced Reading

Voyant Tools is a free, web-accessible suite of in-browser text analysis environments designed to foster dynamic scholarly interpretation[cite: 10, 15]. It does not aim to replace human reading; rather, it invents a computational companion for exploring, querying, and illuminating textual evidence in real time[cite: 10, 15].

“Voyant, the main tool intervention of Hermeneutica, is a newer text-analysis environment meant to support Agile Hermeneutics ↗ (Click for Definition) .” — Geoffrey Rockwell & Stéfan Sinclair (2016), Hermeneutica: Computer-Assisted Interpretation in the Humanities, Chapter 1, p. 10[cite: 10, 15]
“• Voyant is a Web-accessible, Web-based set of tools. ... You can weave embeddable toys into your projects, share them, and use them to work collaboratively.”
“• Voyant is designed to work 'just in time' on any online or uploaded text. ... It combines the capabilities of personal-computer-based pre-indexing tools, such as TACT, with more accessible Web-based tools that can find text and create indexes in real time.”
“• Voyant is designed to fit into the research cycle flexibly. It can export a panel of evidence that can be easily embedded into an online essay just as one can embed a YouTube video into a blog entry.” — Hermeneutica, Chapter 1, p. 11[cite: 10, 15]
“These skins express one of our fundamental beliefs about text analysis: that it is not about replacing interpretation, but about enhanced reading. Voyant is meant to be ready at hand if you want to think through texts. As such, it is just another machine for interpretation, like the codex or the concordance.” — Hermeneutica, Chapter 2, p. 40[cite: 10, 15]
Demystifying the Code

What Technologies Were Used? The Digital Architecture

Instead of viewing code as an intimidating black box, think of Voyant as a four-story intellectual building, translating human speech into interactive architecture[cite: 10, 15]:

LEVEL 4 · THE VISUAL ROOF (Skins & Dynamic Panels) JavaScript / Ext JS / D3.js

What it does in plain words: This is the interactive dashboard you touch with your mouse[cite: 10, 15]. Sencha Ext JS creates draggable, resizable windows, while D3.js paints living word clouds (Cirrus) and trend charts that move whenever you click[cite: 10, 15].

LEVEL 3 · THE SPEED ENGINE (Trombone & Inverted Indexing) Java / Apache Lucene / Trombone

What it does in plain words: The heart of the machine[cite: 10, 15]. When you drop a book or PDF, Apache PDFBox tears the text out, and Apache Lucene creates a lightning-fast dictionary index in milliseconds so you can search through millions of words instantly[cite: 10, 15].

LEVEL 2 · THE BRAIN (Language Statistics & Machine Learning) Stanford CoreNLP / MALLET / JAMA

What it does in plain words: Stanford CoreNLP automatically detects who is a person and what is a place (building social networks in RezoViz)[cite: 10, 15]; MALLET discovers hidden themes across thousands of articles without human intervention[cite: 10, 15].

LEVEL 1 · THE NEW HORIZON (Computational Writing) Spyral Notebook / RESTful APIs

What it does in plain words: Voyant's cutting-edge environment, Spyral, lets you write an essay and run live text calculations right inside the paragraph, creating the ultimate "hybrid scholarly essay"[cite: 10, 15].

Lineage & Governance

Who is Responsible? Funding, Institutions & The Living Consortium

1996 – 2003 · Early Seeds & TAPoR Experiments

Precedents in Canadian text concordancing (PRORA, TACT, HyperPo)[cite: 10, 15]. Geoffrey Rockwell and Stéfan Sinclair launch exploratory experiments within the TAPoR laboratory at McMaster University[cite: 10, 15].

2003 – 2016 · National Grants & The Hermeneutica Milestone

Major research backing from SSHRC (Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council of Canada) and CFI (Canada Foundation for Innovation)[cite: 10, 15]. Co-developed between the University of Alberta and McGill University with lead programmer Andrew MacDonald[cite: 10, 15].

2020 – 2026 · Sustainable Governance & The Voyant Consortium

Following Prof. Sinclair’s passing, the Voyant Consortium was established to safeguard open-source continuity[cite: 10, 15]. Supported by the Digital Research Alliance of Canada and the national LINCS Project (voyant.lincsproject.ca)[cite: 10, 15].

“Agile Hermeneutics and Voyant are therefore self-aware re-inventions built on the giant shoulders of a tradition, not fixed and stable production systems. As research tools, they are unstable models, not finished things.” — Hermeneutica, Chapter 1, p. 12[cite: 10, 15]

Hermeneutical Concept Note

What is "Agile Hermeneutics"?

In Hermeneutica (Rockwell & Sinclair, 2016, Chapter 1, pp. 10–12), Agile Hermeneutics is defined as a nimble, iterative approach to computer-assisted text interpretation[cite: 10, 15]. Inspired by agile software philosophy, it prioritizes:

  • Rapid Cycles: Moving back and forth smoothly between macro-statistical overviews and micro-close reading.
  • Exploratory Play: Treating digital tools as interactive toys for questioning rather than definitive proof engines.
  • Tool Interventions: Embedding computational widgets directly into humanistic arguments.

Reference: Rockwell & Sinclair (2016), Chapter 1, p. 10; Chapter 2, p. 40[cite: 10, 15].
