Nine Days in Suspended Limbo: Bodily Resistance Under Administrative Erasure

Day 1 (May 17, 10:22): The young woman sat in the corridor before the Provost's window, spitting toothpaste onto the concrete floor to scrawl "Iraq" and unknown glyphs[cite: 7]. Later, she stripped mattress covers, severed the water distributor cable, and attempted to conceal a metal spoon in her jacket pocket[cite: 7].
Day 2 (May 17/18, Night): She tore the directional pictogram from the toilet door[cite: 7]. When lectured on internal order, she reacted with indifferent laughter and sat motionless on the corridor floor for hours[cite: 7].
Day 3 & 4 (May 18–19): She scavenged food scraps from garbage bins, carried an iron pan into the ward, kicked the sluice security door, and tipped over the heavy laundry machines[cite: 7]. Locking herself in the cubicle, she wrote "Glass hate" with toothpaste across the window pane[cite: 7].
Day 5 & 7 (May 20–21): Holding four knives at breakfast; letting all water taps run indefinitely; hurling tomatoes and boiled eggs at the guarded gate after an interview with a psychiatrist[cite: 7]. Words appeared on room 041's window: "Death to everyone always" and "Bombs not food altera"[cite: 7].
Day 8 & 9 (May 22–23): Collecting wild mushrooms, toxic leaves, and cigarette butts in her pockets; smearing jam and cheese spread across doors and walls[cite: 7]. The Center Head ordered direct coercive force, manual strip-searching, and permanent transfer into a solitary isolation cell monitored every 15 minutes[cite: 7].

How Voyant Unlocks the Institutional Archive

Across nine official Border Guard memorandums comprising 1,246 Polish words, the female detainee's legal name, personal voice, and inner trauma are utterly excised[cite: 7]. By employing Voyant Tools for distant reading, computational text analysis does not merely calculate frequencies—it makes visible the "External Limbo" of bureaucratic discipline versus the "Internal Limbo" of human struggle, exposing how a total institution reduces a suffering individual to a dangerous, nameless body[cite: 7].

Concept & Philosophy

What is the Point? What Does It Offer? Why Is It Important?

Voyant Tools is a free, web-accessible, in-browser suite designed for digital text analysis, visual exploration, and algorithmic distant reading[cite: 14, 17]. Rather than enforcing rigid computational scripts, it offers instant generation of word clouds (Cirrus), diachronic frequency distribution curves (Trends), and collocational network graphs (Collocates)[cite: 13, 17].

“Voyant, the main tool intervention of Hermeneutica, is a newer text-analysis environment meant to support Agile Hermeneutics.” — Geoffrey Rockwell & Stéfan Sinclair (2016), Hermeneutica: Computer-Assisted Interpretation in the Humanities, Chapter 1, p. 10[cite: 13, 17]

Voyant redefines the relationship between reader and text, establishing an interactive environment of "serious play" and exploratory reflection[cite: 13]:

“• Voyant is a Web-accessible, Web-based set of tools. ... You can weave embeddable toys into your projects, share them, and use them to work collaboratively.”
“• Voyant is designed to work 'just in time' on any online or uploaded text. ... It combines the capabilities of personal-computer-based pre-indexing tools, such as TACT, with more accessible Web-based tools that can find text and create indexes in real time.”
“• Voyant is designed to fit into the research cycle flexibly. It can export a panel of evidence that can be easily embedded into an online essay just as one can embed a YouTube video into a blog entry.” — Hermeneutica, Chapter 1, p. 11[cite: 13, 17]
“These skins express one of our fundamental beliefs about text analysis: that it is not about replacing interpretation, but about enhanced reading. Voyant is meant to be ready at hand if you want to think through texts. As such, it is just another machine for interpretation, like the codex or the concordance.” — Hermeneutica, Chapter 2, p. 40[cite: 13, 17]
Case Study In Action

The Polish Detention Study: Voyant’s Categorical Dissection

In Agnieszka Chwieduk's empirical inquiry ('External Limbo' - 'Internal Limbo': On the Detention of Immigrants in Polish Guarded Centres for Foreigners, 2022), Voyant Tools analyzed the entire 1,246-word corpus of official memos[cite: 7]. Out of 51 terms appearing 3+ times, Voyant’s frequency and collocational algorithms distilled four strictly separated structural categories[cite: 7]:

CATEGORY 1 · PEOPLE

Depersonalized institutional agents & passive subjects[cite: 7]:

umieszczona [detainee]13×
wartownik [sentry guard]9×
cudzoziemka [foreigner]8×
profos [provost]5×
SG [border guard]4×
CATEGORY 2 · ACTIONS

First-person supervisory verbs enforcing protocol[cite: 7]:

zapoznałem [(I) acquainted]12×
przestrzeganiu [comply]7×
zwrócić [pay attention]6×
nakazałem [(I) prescribed]5×
poinformowałem [(I) informed]5×
CATEGORY 3 · SPACES

Confined architectural zones of surveillance[cite: 7]:

ośrodka / SOdC [centre]22×
małoletnich [minors ward]9×
podłodze / podłogę [floor]9×
pokoju [room]7×
izolacyjnym [isolation cell]3×
CATEGORY 4 · OBJECTS

Disruptive material contact points[cite: 7]:

śmieci [rubbish]10×
jedzenie / jedzenia [food]7×
wodę [water]5×
drzwi [door]6×
pokrowce [mattress covers]3×
Collocational Truth (CorpusCollocates): Voyant demonstrated that umieszczona (detainee) directly clusters with śmieci (rubbish, 5×), wodę (water, 4×), toalety (toilet, 4×), and regulaminu (regulations, 4×)[cite: 7]. The quantitative evidence lays bare the moral economy of the center: the woman's psychic agony was officially registered solely as an impediment to cleanliness and architectural order[cite: 7].
The Digital Architecture

What Technologies Were Used/Relevant?

Building digital humanities tools is akin to crafting architecture or mapping uncharted territory[cite: 14, 17]. Translating raw natural language into computational matrices turns intangible human discourse into interactive virtual instruments[cite: 14, 17]:

Level 4 · Visual Skins & Interactive Frontend JavaScript (ES6+) / Ext JS / D3.js

Driven by Sencha Ext JS for customizable, dockable multi-panel desktop layouts (Cirrus, Trends, Bubblelines), combined with D3.js for interactive vector graphics, real-time node links, and Google Closure Compiler script optimization[cite: 14, 17].

Level 3 · Computational Engine & Indexing Core Java (Java EE) / Apache Lucene / Trombone

Powered by the proprietary Trombone engine built upon Apache Lucene[cite: 14, 17]. Executes inverted text indexing, tokenization, and dynamic n-gram calculations in milliseconds[cite: 13, 14, 17]. Employs Apache PDFBox and POI to ingest PDFs, DOCX, and HTML raw inputs[cite: 14, 17].

Level 2 · NLP, Machine Learning & Statistics Stanford CoreNLP / MALLET / JAMA

Embeds Stanford CoreNLP for Named Entity Recognition (powering RezoViz social network graphs), MALLET for Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) Topic Modeling, and JAMA for multidimensional Correspondence Analysis[cite: 13, 14, 17].

Level 1 · Modern Ecosystem & Web Notebook Spyral Notebook / RESTful Web API

Provides Spyral—the next-generation computational notebook that bridges executable JavaScript analysis with narrative scholarly essays, alongside REST APIs supporting single-panel iframe embeddings[cite: 10, 14, 17].

Agenda & Philosophy

What Was the Point of the Project? What Was Their Agenda?

“Voyant allows users to integrate interpretation into their writing by creating small hermeneutica that can be woven into their essays or into Web 2.0 online writing environments such as blogs and wikis.” — Hermeneutica, Chapter 1, p. 2[cite: 13, 17]
“However, in practice we found it difficult to move swiftly from the interpretive tool environment (in which textual evidence is explored) to the environment of the essay (in which a new interpretation is crafted). In response to that difficulty, we created Voyant.” — Hermeneutica, Chapter 1, pp. 22–23[cite: 13, 17]
“Formalization, not quantification, is the foundation of computer-assisted interpretation. We use the computer to model a text in both the sense of creating a representation and in the sense of manipulating that representation by creating interpretive tools that allow us to do both.” — Hermeneutica, Chapter 2, p. 41[cite: 13, 17]
“Hermeneutica proposes an alternative approach: the hybrid essay, an interpretive work embedded with hermeneutical toys that can be explored for technique. ... Hermeneutica embedded in traditional forms of scholarly arguments offer more than a hybrid approach; they offer a more engaging approach.” — Hermeneutica, Chapter 5, p. 17 / p. 97[cite: 13, 17]
“Should artificial intelligence arise, we will still invent (or re-invent) tools that allow us to use our own intelligence. We will still develop interpretive tools—hermeneutica—that can augment and extend our reading, not replace us.” — Hermeneutica, Chapter 2, p. 43[cite: 13, 17]
Governance & Lineage

Who is Responsible? Institutions, Funding & Operational Status

Voyant Tools is sustained through a long-standing collaborative ecology centered in Canadian digital humanities scholarship[cite: 13, 14]:

1966 – 2003 · Precedents & TAPoR Lab Experiments

Originating from Canadian text concording traditions (PRORA, TACT, HyperPo), the foundational prototype was incubated in the TAPoR lab at McMaster University, spearheaded by Geoffrey Rockwell and Stéfan Sinclair[cite: 13, 14].

2003 – 2016 · National Grants & Hermeneutica (MIT Press)

Funded by major grants from the Canada Foundation for Innovation (CFI) and the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council of Canada (SSHRC)[cite: 13, 14]. Co-hosted by the University of Alberta (Arts Resource Centre) and McGill University (Centre for Digital Humanities), with infrastructure from Compute Canada (WestGrid, Sharcnet) and technical leadership by Andrew MacDonald[cite: 13, 14].

2020 – 2026 · Voyant Consortium & Sustainable Governance

Following Prof. Sinclair's passing in 2020, governance transitioned to the Voyant Consortium, operating under formal bylaws with two Co-Directors, an Executive Board, a Community Board, and a Technical Committee[cite: 10, 11, 12]. Hosted at the University of Alberta and powered by the Digital Research Alliance of Canada and the LINCS Project (voyant.lincsproject.ca)[cite: 10, 11].

“Agile Hermeneutics and Voyant are therefore self-aware re-inventions built on the giant shoulders of a tradition, not fixed and stable production systems. As research tools, they are unstable models, not finished things.” — Hermeneutica, Chapter 1, p. 12[cite: 13, 17]
